# Caisson Lift Sweep — Synthesis Report

## Executive summary

Six paused GridWork repos were scouted (with adversarial critic + second-pass correction) for code, patterns, and product ideas worth rebuilding clean into `@caisson/*`. None of the six is reusable wholesale — **telesis**, **gridwork**, and **gridworkdigital** are the strongest donors (production-hardened compliance/tenancy/billing plumbing buried under consumer-app or agency-SaaS business logic); **throughframe** and **glossread** are early/spec-mature AI-production repos whose value is concentrated in eval/cost-governance/guardrail _patterns_ rather than finished infra; **tm-watch** is a pure documents-only NO-GO repo whose only export is a handful of well-researched compliance-design lessons. After critic verification, **37 raw "high-value" candidates** were flagged across the six repos; **9 were downgraded** on second pass (mostly cases where caisson's existing `ai-meter`/`audit-chain`/`compliance` packages are already structurally ahead, or where a candidate was boilerplate misdressed as engineering). The headline lifts: a **complete governed-agent-kernel reference** (telesis/hermes) with a billing-mode refusal guard caisson likely lacks; a **fully-built SOC2 CC6 privileged-access (support impersonation) + CCPA/GDPR erasure-runner pair** (gridworkdigital) with nothing comparable elsewhere in the survey; a **Wilson-CI-gated, budget-isolated eval harness** (telesis) that is the single closest 1:1 match to `ai-evals`' stated purpose; and two **genuinely absent ai-evals/ai-meter capabilities** surfaced only by throughframe — a production-disagreement goldset accumulator (today's `ai-evals` is static-only) and a pre-call MinHash dedup gate (today's `ai-kit`/`ai-meter` pipeline metering everything has no "have we already paid for something this similar" check).

## Where caisson is already AHEAD (do not downgrade caisson)

- **`ai-meter`** (`breaker.ts`/`meter.ts`): persistent, RLS-scoped, fail-closed Postgres circuit breaker surviving restarts, atomic spend-window bumps, reserve()-before/reconcile()-after against actual provider usage, idempotent on `call_id` — strictly ahead of telesis's `ExtractBudget` (in-process float, copy-pasted 3x, resets every run) and throughframe's `emit_cost_event` (best-effort, silently drops on failure).
- **`audit-chain.ts`**: canonicalize() + SHA-256 chain + `verifyChain(entries, anchor)` detecting tail-truncation/wholesale-rewrite via a trusted anchor — ahead of throughframe's flat unanchored content-hash provenance stamp.
- **`compliance/evidence/generate.ts` + `pack-format.ts`**: flag-never-guess assembly (a partial/leaky pack is structurally impossible, not just size-bounded), deterministic byte-stable ZIPs, typed `EvidencePackBlockedError` — ahead of throughframe's `staging.py` bounded-truncated-reject-record approach.
- **audit-worm/audit-chain design generally**: gridworkdigital's impersonation/operator-action/retention audit tables are plain Postgres rows with no hash-chaining or append-only enforcement — good audit-_logging_ discipline, not WORM. gridwork's own `audit-log.ts` (mutable, 200-entry-capped KV array) is explicitly worse and was flagged as negative evidence, not a lift.
- **field-crypto**: no KMS-integration was found in any surveyed repo (telesis's own source comment flags this as future work) — caisson should already be ahead if it has KMS-backed key wrapping.
- **General theme**: every "X-as-WORM" or "X-as-audit-evidence" candidate surfaced by the critic pass turned out to be ordinary audit-logging dressed up as tamper-evidence — a recurring confusion worth flagging to future scouts, not a capability gap.
