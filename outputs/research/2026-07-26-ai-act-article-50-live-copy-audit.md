# EU AI Act Article 50 live-copy audit

## Verdict

The three live surfaces contain **45 distinct regulatory or legal-proof claim units**:

- **14 ACCURATE**
- **20 INCOMPLETE**
- **11 WRONG**

The highest-risk error is the categorical answer at
`apps/site/app/frameworks/eu-ai-act/article-50/page.tsx:69-71`. It says “No” to whether the
August date was delayed and then presents all Article 50 obligations as enforceable from
2 August 2026. The general application date remains 2 August, but the final Commission
guidelines identify a targeted transition to **2 December 2026** for Article 50(2)'s marking and
detection obligation when a generative AI system was placed on the market or put into service
before 2 August.

The lede at `apps/site/app/frameworks/eu-ai-act/article-50/page.tsx:148-149` is not wholly false:
the general date did not move. It is **INCOMPLETE** because it uses that fact to summarize the
post-Omnibus position without the targeted Article 50(2) transition.

Two other patterns need correction:

1. Several lines say a tamper-evident record “proves” the Article 50 obligation was met. Article
   50 requires disclosure, marking, detection, clarity, timing, and accessibility. The primary
   sources do not make a WORM or hash-chained record a compliance condition.
2. Two lines present marking “at the generation boundary” as the required implementation. The
   final guidelines expressly permit marking at different value-chain stages, including post hoc,
   at the underlying model, or in the inference process.

This file proposes copy only. It makes no change to `apps/site`.

## Classification

- **ACCURATE** — matches the primary sources without a material omission in its immediate
  context.
- **INCOMPLETE** — contains a true core statement but omits a role, condition, exception,
  transition, or second element needed to avoid misleading the reader.
- **WRONG** — contradicts the primary source, turns an optional implementation into a legal
  requirement, or claims a record proves legal satisfaction when the sources do not.

The audit covers rendered copy, metadata, JSON-LD, and source constants that render into the
page. Non-rendered comments are excluded. Product-capability claims are excluded unless they
change the stated legal requirement or claim to prove it.
