### What this says about single-operator feasibility

- **No vendor in this set sells a pure standalone "support SKU"** except Keygen's $995/mo add-on (gated to already-paying Enterprise customers, not sold alone). Everyone else folds support into a plan-tier upgrade (Metabase/Prisma/Supabase/Chatwoot pattern) or excludes it (Tailwind Plus, Sidekiq's soft version).
- **The two actual solo/small-team comparables both hedge hard.** Sidekiq (one person, years of solo maintenance) caps paid support at **1 incident/quarter, 2-working-day soft ceiling, no contract, scope-limited to the library itself** — the most honest ceiling in the set for what one person can promise. Tailwind Plus (2-3 person team) doesn't sell support at all — they narrow scope to "self-serve" instead of overselling responsiveness.
- **Business-hours-only, weekdays-only coverage is the market norm below Enterprise** even at large-vendor scale (Prisma 9-5 CET, Metabase 8-5 Pacific, n8n 9-5 Berlin/NY). 24/7 response targets only appear at Supabase Team ($599/mo, VC-backed multi-person support org) and above.
- **Contractual SLAs with credit/remedy clauses are rare outside uptime.** Response-time numbers are usually stated as a firm target in a support policy page, not backed by a penalty clause. Only uptime SLAs (Supabase, Keygen) carry actual credit formulas.

### Candidate structures for the priority-support SKU

**A — "Sidekiq model": rationed, best-effort, cheap**
Flat monthly or annual add-on. Capped incident count (e.g. "N priority incidents per period"), a soft business-days response ceiling (not hours), explicitly scoped to the library/module itself (not the buyer's app or infra), no contractual credit/remedy language. Priced as a small multiple of the cheapest bundle, not tied to which bundle was purchased.

- _Trade-off:_ cheapest to sell honestly and to actually deliver solo; weakest as a differentiator since it barely beats what committed buyers already expect from GitHub issues.
- _Feasibility:_ highest — this is what Sidekiq's own solo-maintainer years validated as sustainable. Lowest legal/ops exposure (no credits owed on a miss).

**B — "Keygen model": firm hours, flat fee, no credit backstop**
A specific stated response-time number (e.g. "within N business hours") on a defined weekday/business-hours window, sold as a flat recurring add-on regardless of which bundle was bought, gated to buyers who already hold an entitlement (mirrors Keygen restricting the add-on to Enterprise-tier customers). No uptime-style credit formula attached to the response-time number — the number is a target, not a penalty-backed guarantee.

- _Trade-off:_ stronger marketing claim than A (a real hours number, not "we'll get to it"), but riskier if volume spikes — a solo operator publishing "N business hours" and missing it repeatedly is a reputational cost even without a contractual penalty.
- _Feasibility:_ medium — doable if volume caps or an incident-count ceiling (borrowed from Sidekiq) rides alongside the hours number, so the commitment is bounded on both axes (speed AND count).

**C — "Plan-tier model": fold into a higher updates-window tier, no standalone SKU**
Instead of a separate line item, priority support becomes a benefit of a richer subscription tier (mirrors Metabase Pro→Enterprise and Prisma Standard→Premium: same product, faster support as the tier's differentiator) — sitting next to Caisson's existing Developer updates-plan structure (ADR-0272 §5 lineage, the 40%-of-list renewal anchor) rather than as its own Paddle product.

- _Trade-off:_ less catalog complexity (no new SKU/entitlement id/dashboard row), but ADR-0278 explicitly asked for "a priority-support subscription SKU" as its own commercial surface — this option partially contradicts the ADR's stated decision shape and would need a frame amendment, not just a number.
- _Feasibility:_ high operationally, but scope mismatch with the locked ADR frame — flag this one to the operator as "does the frame still hold" rather than a clean third option.

**Range signal from the comparables:** support add-ons cluster at **~2.6x-5.2x** the price of the tier below them when sold as a tier upgrade (Prisma 2.6x, Metabase 2.9x, Chatwoot 5.2x per-seat), and Supabase's 24x outlier is a VC-scale support org, not a comparable ceiling for one person. Applied loosely to Caisson's own bundle floor ($329) that ratio band is illustrative only — **the operator sets the actual number**; the honest ceiling on _promise_ (not price) is Sidekiq's "1 incident/quarter, 2 business days, no contract."

### Negative finding — the well-known dev-infra "startup programs" are a different animal

Modal Labs, Baseten, Temporal, Neon, Convex, Clerk, PlanetScale, WarpStream, Braintrust: **none** publish a case-study-for-discount design-partner program. What they publish is VC-network-gated **infra credit grants** ($400-$100K credits, funding-stage-gated) aimed at already-funded companies, with no feedback-cadence or case-study obligation attached. This is a structurally different motion (acquisition funnel for funded startups) from what ADR-0273 is building (first citable logos, pre-launch, zero customers). Not a usable comparable for term-setting — noted so the operator doesn't anchor on it by mistake.

### Candidate structures for the design-partner program

**A — "SaaStr/Allston Labs standard": term-limited discount, contingent case study, capped cohort**
30-50% off list for a fixed 12-24 month window, reverting to standard pricing at the end unless a separate renewal decision is made. Case-study/logo rights **granted at signing but contingent on conversion** (Allston Labs' finding: this yields more usable logos than asking post-hoc). Biweekly or monthly feedback call, firm cadence but capped hours. Cohort capped at 3-5 (SaaStr's explicit ceiling, matches ADR-0273's own "~3-5 partners" framing).

- _Trade-off:_ the most evidence-backed structure (appears across SaaStr, Allston Labs, Koji.so, and matches ADR-0273's stated cohort size already) but requires tracking a reversion date per partner — one more thing a solo operator has to remember to action.
- _Feasibility:_ high — the cadence and cohort size are both within what one person can sustain (SVPG's own six-partner number matches; Allston Labs' 4-8hr/mo founder time cap for the first 90 days is a real one-person budget).

**B — "Sierra/BVP model": paid upfront, short fixed window, hard conversion deadline**
Partners pay something upfront (not free/deeply-discounted access) for a short, fixed window (First Round's Sierra case: 3 months, paid upfront, weekly standups, 100% conversion). The engagement ends in a binary go/no-go — no indefinite "still in pilot" state (BVP's explicit warning: "indefinite free access is an advisory relationship, not a validation signal").

- _Trade-off:_ strongest signal quality (real money paid = real reference customer per SVPG's strict bar) and forces a clean end date, but a pre-launch product with zero references may struggle to get anyone to pay upfront before the first case study exists — chicken-and-egg risk this specific ADR context (zero reference customers pre-launch) makes worse than it was for Sierra (which already had traction).
- _Feasibility:_ medium — weekly standups for 3-5 partners over 3 months is sustainable solo, but recruiting paid-upfront partners with zero social proof is the harder lift, not the support burden.

**C — "Founding-member model": deep discount locked forever, light-touch ask, continuous-subscription condition**
30-50% off (or free bundle, per ADR-0273's own framing) locked permanently, but **conditioned on continuous ownership/active status** (the universal pattern across all 7 founding-member SaaS comparables) rather than a term that reverts. Case-study ask is lightweight — logo/name listed as a founding partner, full written case study optional/best-effort rather than mandatory. No firm feedback-call cadence — async/Slack access instead.

- _Trade-off:_ lowest operator burden (no calls to run, no reversion date to track) but weakest on the specific goal ADR-0273 states — "citable reference stories" and "integration-fit proof" need an actual case study and feedback loop, and this structure doesn't reliably produce either. Best suited if the program's real goal is early revenue + goodwill rather than case-study production.
- _Feasibility:_ highest operationally, lowest on the ADR's actual stated purpose (reference stories) — flag this mismatch to the operator rather than defaulting to it for being easiest.

**Range signal from the comparables:** discount depth clusters tightly at **30-50% off** across nearly every source (SaaStr, Allston Labs "typically 50-90% or free," Koji.so, Unusual VC, the founding-member SaaS set) — this is the market center of gravity, not an edge case. Duration clusters at **1-3 months for a paid/converting motion (Sierra, Focal's "2-month sweet spot")** vs **12-24 months for a discount-then-reverts motion (SaaStr, Koji.so)**. Cohort size clusters at **3-6** across every source that states a number (SaaStr's 3-5 ceiling, SVPG's canonical six, Allston Labs' 3 signed from a 30-lead funnel) — **ADR-0273's own "~3-5 partners" framing already sits inside the evidence-backed range**, so this is confirmatory, not a new finding.

## Gaps

- Retool, WorkOS, Trigger.dev, Inngest, Unkey: no public design-partner-program _terms_ pages found — these companies likely ran the motion privately pre-launch. Not a data gap that blocks the memo (the 17 sources gathered are sufficient for range-setting) but flagged for completeness.
- Sentry, Directus (self-service tier), n8n: no public numeric response-time SLA at all for their entry paid tiers — that opacity is itself reported as a data point above, not treated as a missing row.
- No PostHog funnel data or Cookiy WTP survey run for this pass — out of scope for this dispatch (comparables-only request); if the operator wants funnel/WTP evidence layered on top of these ranges before locking numbers, that's a separate follow-up.

## Operator decides

This memo sets no price and no term. Priority-support SKU: the honest single-operator
ceiling on _promise_ sits closest to Sidekiq's rationed/best-effort model; the pricing
ratio band from tier-upgrade comparables is ~2.6x-5.2x (Supabase's 24x is a VC-scale
outlier, not a ceiling). Design-partner program: discount depth clusters 30-50% off,
cohort size 3-6 (ADR-0273's own ~3-5 framing already sits in-range), duration splits
between a short paid/converting window (1-3mo) and a longer discount-then-reverts window
(12-24mo) depending on which structure the operator prefers. Final numbers and structure
choice are operator-owned.
