# WTP memo — Kickoff-J D2/D3 pricing-picker input

## TL;DR

1. **No real ICP buyer has ever reacted to any Caisson price.** Every quant leg (Van
   Westendorp 445432, frame test 776545) is 62–68% off-ICP "Other" role respondents,
   and the qual corpus's one real buyer who reached a concept never saw a dollar
   figure. This caps everything below — treat every number as directional, not
   validated WTP (`wtp-synthesis-partial-2026-07-09.md` §2, `cookiy-deep-analysis`
   Lens 3).
2. **F1 (anchor level): hold, don't cut, weak case for room up — not a case to raise
   now.** Qual + a directional quant ladder both point the same way; comparables
   ceiling ($1,276–1,499) and AuditKit's live $99–999/mo subscription wedge bound the
   range on both sides. Confidence: medium.
3. **F2 (seat allowance): a real market-shape gap flagged by comparables, zero WTP
   evidence either way.** Local-first $629 / AI-Production $739 are single-seat SKUs
   sitting in a market band that reportedly buys ≤5 seats. Confidence: low — the
   comparable figures need a fresh scrape before this fork locks.
4. **F3 (à-la-carte posture): price-high-steer-to-bundle stands on zero contradicting
   evidence, but "zero evidence" is a gap, not a validation** — no synthetic or real
   respondent in 45 qual transcripts or 60 quant completes ever reacted to a module
   price.
5. **F4 (renewal ratio): hold the 40%/X9 ratio — this is the strongest-converged
   fork in the whole memo.** Qual (zero pushback across 40 synthetics) and quant (58%
   fair-or-pay, ICP subset 7/9) agree; the live objection is 12-month-cliff copy, already
   partially shipped. Confidence: high on "don't touch the ratio," medium on "copy is
   fully closed."
6. **The standing data gap has one named closer**: a ≥5-interview real-ICP qual round
   that reaches the pricing screen (`cookiy-deep-analysis` Lens 2 §7, Lens 3 "Data
   gaps"). Nothing below should be read as license to skip it.

## Evidence base + validity tiers

Every claim in this memo is tagged to one of four tiers. The operator is pricing real
money on this corpus — the tiering is the point, not a caveat to skim.

| Tier                                                | What it is                                                                                                                                                           | What it can prove                                                                                                                                                                                  | What it cannot prove                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Qual-final, synthetic-dominant**                  | 45 Cookiy transcripts (40 synthetic personas + 5 real humans), independently re-analyzed across 4 lenses + an accuracy critic (`cookiy-deep-analysis-2026-07-07.md`) | Directional buyer-language patterns, objection clusters, what a well-specified persona says when shown the anchor                                                                                  | Real willingness-to-pay — "$1,049 seems reasonable" from 40 copies of one archetype is LLM agreeableness, not market signal (Lens 2 §5.2, explicit in the source) |
| **Frame test — within-subject, composition-robust** | Cookiy survey 776545, 37/60 complete, every respondent saw both framings                                                                                             | A real preference/clarity delta between two positioning frames, robust to an off-ICP panel because the comparison is within-subject                                                                | Absolute price levels, ICP-specific WTP — the panel is 68.4% "Other" role                                                                                         |
| **Ladder — directional, off-ICP**                   | Cookiy survey 445432 price-sensitivity ladder (not open-ended VW), 26/60 complete                                                                                    | That presentation/anchoring changes gut price reactions in the same off-ICP panel whose open-ended VW numbers are consumer-frame garbage — a directional signal about anchoring, not a price point | Any crossing-point, any ICP-specific number — clean ICP rows are n=3                                                                                              |
| **Zero real-ICP price reaction — the standing gap** | The fact itself                                                                                                                                                      | Nothing — this is the negative-space finding that bounds every claim above                                                                                                                         | —                                                                                                                                                                 |

**The open-ended VW numbers are explicitly unusable and are not cited as evidence
anywhere below** — `wtp-synthesis-partial-2026-07-09.md` §4 states the clean-subset
medians (TC $5/CH $15/EX $33/TE $45) are "consumer-frame" and instructs "do not compute
or cite VW crossing points from this leg — at any fill level." This memo follows that
instruction; only the price-ladder read (rungs shown, not generated) is used, and it is
tagged directional-only per the table above.

## Fork-by-fork analysis

### F1 — D3 anchor level: Compliance $1,049 / Everything $2,059

Committed today (`docs/gtm/pricing-packaging.md`, live in `apps/site/lib/pricing.ts`):
Compliance $1,049, Everything $2,059 (ADR-0258).

**Options:**

- **(a) Hold both numbers as-is.**
- **(b) Raise** — either bundle, toward the comparables ceiling.
- **(c) Cut** — either bundle, toward a subscription-competitive entry point.

**Evidence:**

- Qual: zero price resistance across 40 synthetic reactions; every WTP proxy volunteered
  (3–6 month internal builds, ~$15k quotes) sits far above $1,049; a latent
  too-cheap-to-trust skepticism ("sounds like a silver bullet... skepticism," `syn-019f3af9-a6fc`)
  argues the binding constraint is proof + terms, not price level (`cookiy-deep-analysis`
  Lens 3 "Direct read on the open forks," D2). The one real qual data point cuts the other
  way but never saw a number: `real-019f3b34` named price a "nonnegotiable blocker" in the
  abstract, cost-consciousness framing, with no dollar figure shown (Lens 2 §3e, and the
  accuracy critic flags the "nonnegotiable blocker" phrase as interviewer-scaffolded — the
  buyer filled in "pricing" to a leading question, so weight this as a caution flag, not a
  clean anchor).
- Quant ladder (`wtp-synthesis-partial-2026-07-09.md` §4): 58% rate $1,049 fair-or-would-consider,
  vs. 69% at $649 and 46% at $1,499 — the same panel whose unanchored "too expensive" median
  was $45. The finding is the _divergence_, not the absolute numbers: presentation dominates
  gut pricing in this panel, which is directional support for holding the anchor and pairing
  it with a build-cost comparator, not for reading 58% as a market-clearing rate.
- Comparables (SYNTHESIS.md §2): "Defensible; room UP not down" — ceiling comps $1,276–1,499
  (Gravity Elite, Supastarter Agency) carry commodity feature sets; zero comps ship
  WORM/field-crypto/OSCAL at any price.
- Competitive counter-pressure (`market-intel-2026-07-09.md` Ranked #2, crawled 2026-07-09):
  **AuditKit.dev is a live, named, paying competitor** on the compliance wedge — SHA-256
  hash-chained audit logs + SOC2 evidence packs at $99–999/mo pure subscription, tagline
  "80% Cheaper vs Vanta/Drata." It doesn't compete on code ownership, but it establishes a
  real subscription floor a price-sensitive buyer can choose instead of a $1,049 one-time
  spend. This is new evidence since the qual/quant legs were designed and neither accounts
  for it.
- The never-raise-on-synthetic-alone caveat is explicit in the source
  (`cookiy-deep-analysis` D2: "Weak synthetic-only evidence of headroom above — do not raise
  on it alone").

**Recommendation: (a) hold, at medium confidence.** The two qual+quant legs that exist both
read as "don't cut" with a weak, synthetic-only whisper of room up; AuditKit's live
subscription wedge is a reason for caution against raising further, not a reason to cut
(different buyer motion — subscription vs. owned-code). This is a "don't touch it on this
evidence" call, not a "this is the right number" call.

**What would change it:** a live real-ICP price reaction in either direction (the ≥5-interview
qual round below); a measurable checkout-funnel signal once commerce is live and price is
the named drop-off reason (none exists pre-launch — see Data gaps); a second AuditKit-shaped
competitor entering with code-ownership parity at a lower price would argue for (c).

### F2 — D2 seat allowance: Local-first $629 / AI-Production $739

**Options:**

- **(a) Add a seat allowance** to these two bundles (e.g., grant N seats before an add-on
  applies) — a packaging change, not a price change.
- **(b) Hold single-seat, add counter-copy** that reframes the comparison (e.g., anchor
  against per-org code ownership rather than per-seat SaaS pricing).
- **(c) Hold as-is, no copy change** — accept the comparison friction as a minor/unproven cost.

**Evidence:**

- SYNTHESIS.md §2 flags this directly: "Flag: seat mismatch — Market's $599–799 buys ≤5
  seats; consider a seat allowance at these tiers or copy that pre-empts the comparison."
  This is the _only_ source in this memo's corpus that raises the seat question — it does
  not appear in either qual or quant WTP leg (no respondent in 45 transcripts or 60 survey
  completes reacted to seat scope).
- The specific comparable figures ("$599–799, ≤5 seats") are not independently re-verified
  in this dispatch — `market-intel-2026-07-09.md` (crawled 2026-07-09, this cycle) shows
  MakerKit's *current* live pricing as $349/$649 lifetime with no seat count stated, a
  different SKU shape than the seat-banded comparable SYNTHESIS.md cites. This is a
  staleness/scope gap, not a contradiction resolved — flag before locking.
- What a seat grant would interact with, named but **not scoped or proposed as engineering
  here**: the license/entitlement resolver (`docs/build-state.md` lineage — expandEntitlements,
  per-org `account_member`, ADR-0170–0176 D4 org accounts), and EULA seat-scope language
  (the licensing-ambiguity objection cluster is the corpus's single most explicit hard veto —
  `cookiy-deep-analysis` Lens 4, cluster 5, "our legal team would likely veto the purchase" on
  redistribution/scope ambiguity, `af8-e972`).

**Recommendation: (b) hold single-seat, add counter-copy, at low confidence — genuinely a
coin flip pending fresh comparable data.** No WTP evidence bears on this fork either way; it
is purely a comparables-framing question, and the comparable data itself needs a re-scrape
before the fork should lock. This is the fork this memo is least equipped to resolve.

**What would change it:** a fresh, dated crawl4ai scrape of the actual seat-inclusive
competitor tiers (MakerKit Team plan specifically, Supastarter's current pricing — the
market-intel run flagged Supastarter as unverified this cycle, crawl4ai 500'd); any qual/quant
signal naming seat count as a purchase blocker (none exists today).

### F3 — À-la-carte module posture

**Options:**

- **(a) Hold price-high-steer-to-bundle** (current posture).
- **(b) Lower select module prices** to compete on entry-point purchases.

**Evidence:**

- SYNTHESIS.md §2: "Price high, steer to bundle — Only precedents are $1–3 convenience
  add-ons (DiggaByte); anchoring anywhere near that undercuts the bundle story; module
  prices should make the bundle read as the obvious deal."
- `cookiy-deep-analysis` Lens 3, ranked action #6 and "Data gaps": modular-preference personas
  exist (compliance-scoped buyers repeatedly prefer "just the compliance bundle" over the
  full substrate) but **"à-la-carte module prices drew zero reactions in the entire sample
  — a genuine data gap, not a validation."** Repeated verbatim in the "Direct read on the
  open forks" section as data gap #2.
- The quant legs add nothing here — neither survey tested a module price point.

**Recommendation: (a) hold, at medium confidence.** The posture is unopposed by every source
consulted, but "unopposed" here means "never tested," not "validated." This is the fork with
the thinnest evidence in either direction — the recommendation rests on comparables logic
(no precedent for a module priced meaningfully below the bundle-steer band) rather than any
buyer reaction.

**What would change it:** any module-price reaction in a future qual/quant round — currently
absent everywhere in the corpus.

### F4 — Renewal ratio (40%/X9) + $499/yr Developer plan

**Options:**

- **(a) Hold the ratio and number, close the remaining copy gap.**
- **(b) Change the ratio or the Developer-plan price.**

**Evidence:**

- Qual: the 40%/$499 ratio "drew zero pushback anywhere" (`cookiy-deep-analysis` Lens 3
  "Renewal intent (D3 core)") — every synthetic reaction that reaches renewal frames it as a
  retrospective value call, never objects to the number itself.
- Quant: 58% fair-or-would-pay on the updates model overall (27% fair, 31% a-bit-high-but-pay,
  31% stay-on-purchased, 12% different model); ICP subset 7/9 fair-or-pay
  (`wtp-synthesis-partial-2026-07-09.md` §4). Convergence matrix (§5 of that source) marks this
  claim "Converges — the stay-on-purchased third is exactly who the perpetual + patch-continuity
  copy serves."
- **The objection cluster, both legs agree, is 12-month-cliff _ambiguity_, not the ratio.**
  Qual: "the single largest cluster" (25/40 synthetics, `cookiy-deep-analysis` "What survives" #2)
  is continuity/post-12-month clarity — a terms problem, not a price problem, answerable with
  existing assets (perpetual-source-you-keep + the live dashboard updates-window, ADR-0260 §5,
  already shipped per `docs/gtm/pricing-packaging.md` "Updates window" section). Quant: a survey
  radio can't test ambiguity directly, but the 31% "stay on purchased version" cell is
  consistent with the same population, not contradicting it.
- Comparables (SYNTHESIS.md §2): "Beats Makerkit's ~50%; but 'lifetime updates included' is
  the category default (6/9 comps) — the subscription must visibly buy more than updates."

**Recommendation: (a) hold the ratio and the $499/yr number, at high confidence** — this is
the strongest convergence in the memo, qual and quant independently landing on the same
read. The residual work is **copy, not price**: naming the specific gaps as pointers, not new
work — the pricing-page "what happens after 12 months" answer (ranked action #1 in
`cookiy-deep-analysis` Lens 3) and the dashboard updates-window surfacing (already shipped per
ADR-0260 §5) are the two named closers. Confidence on "the copy gap is fully closed" is
medium — this memo did not re-audit the live pricing page copy to confirm both are shipped
as described.

**What would change it:** any qual/quant signal naming the _ratio itself_ (not the ambiguity)
as an objection — none exists in either source.

### F5 — Design-partner composition (ADR-0297)

ADR-0297 locks 40% off the partner's initial purchase, 12-month reverting, for the first 5
design partners — "the D2/D3 bundle price levels stay independently HELD... the partner
discount composes on whatever those land at" (ADR-0297 "Consequences"). Applying 40% off to
the current committed matrix, for reference only — **this section computes composition, it
does not recommend a D2/D3 change:**

| Bundle        | List   | Partner (60% of list) |
| ------------- | ------ | --------------------- |
| Agentic-Dev   | $329   | $197.40               |
| Provenance    | $399   | $239.40               |
| Local-first   | $629   | $377.40               |
| AI-Production | $739   | $443.40               |
| Compliance    | $1,049 | $629.40               |
| Everything    | $2,059 | $1,235.40             |

**Flagged collision:** partner-price Compliance ($629.40) lands within a few dollars of
**list-price Local-first ($629)** — a design partner buying the flagship compliance bundle at
the partner rate pays almost exactly what a non-partner buyer pays for a completely different
bundle. This is a copy/positioning awkwardness (a partner-facing pricing table that shows
"$629" next to two different products) rather than a pricing error, and it is **specific to
the current $1,049/$629 pair** — any F1 move on Compliance or F2 move on Local-first changes
or removes this collision. Worth flagging to whoever builds the partner-facing pricing
surface (ADR-0272 site wave), not something this memo resolves.

No other partner price point collides with a list price in the current matrix.

## Operator decides

This memo sets no price. F1 (anchor level) and F4 (renewal ratio) both read as **hold**, at
medium and high confidence respectively. F2 (seat allowance) and F3 (à-la-carte posture) have
no WTP evidence in either direction and rest on comparables data that itself needs
re-verification before either fork should lock. Every number above is a finding or a
composed reference table, never a final call — **forks lock as operator ADRs.**
