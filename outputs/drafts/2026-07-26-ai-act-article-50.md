# EU AI Act Article 50 transparency: a primary-source explainer

> **Scope:** This is a primary-source explainer, not legal advice. The source reading is current
> to 26 July 2026.

Article 50's transparency obligations apply from **2 August 2026**.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 113, OJ p. 123](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

The transition to **2 December 2026** is narrower: it concerns Article 50(2)'s
machine-readable marking and detectability duty for generative AI systems placed on the market
or put into service before 2 August 2026.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), pp. 49–50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

It is not a postponement of Article 50 as a whole.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), pp. 49–50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

## Four transparency duties

Article 50 contains four duties for different systems and outputs.
[Commission guidelines, section 2.1, paragraph (6), pp. 3–4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

### Direct interaction

Providers of AI systems intended to interact directly with people must design them so the people
concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(1), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

The duty does not apply when the AI nature of the interaction is obvious to a reasonably
well-informed, observant, and circumspect person in the circumstances and context of use.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(1), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Article 50(1) also contains a qualified exception for systems authorised by law to detect,
prevent, investigate, or prosecute criminal offences.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(1), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

### Synthetic content

Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, that generate synthetic audio,
image, video, or text must ensure that their outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and
detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(2), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

The technical solutions must be effective, interoperable, robust, and reliable as far as
technically feasible, taking account of the content type, implementation cost, and generally
acknowledged state of the art.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(2), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

That duty does not apply to the extent a system performs an assistive function for standard
editing or does not substantially alter the deployer's input data or its semantics.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(2), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Article 50(2) also contains a qualified law-enforcement exception.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(2), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

### Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation

Deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems must inform the people
exposed to the operation of the system and process personal data under the applicable Union data
protection rules.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(3), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Article 50(3) contains a qualified law-enforcement exception.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(3), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

### Deepfakes and public-interest text

Deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate image, audio, or video constituting a
deepfake must disclose that the content was artificially generated or manipulated.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4), first subparagraph, OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

For evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional, or analogous works, the required
disclosure is limited to an appropriate form that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the
work.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4), first subparagraph, OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Deployers that publish AI-generated or manipulated text to inform the public on matters of public
interest must disclose that artificial generation or manipulation.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4), second subparagraph, OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

That public-interest-text duty does not apply when the content underwent human review or
editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the
publication.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4), second subparagraph, OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Both parts of Article 50(4) also contain qualified law-enforcement exceptions.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4), OJ p. 82](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Information required by Article 50(1)–(4) must be clear and distinguishable, supplied no later
than the first interaction or exposure, and conform to applicable accessibility requirements.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(5), OJ p. 83](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

## The two dates

The Commission's quick-facts page describes a grace period until December 2026 for the Article
50(2) marking obligation of generative AI systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026.
[Commission quick facts, “Enforcement and penalties” → “Exceptions”](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems)

The final guidelines give the exact boundary: the targeted rule covers Article 50(2)'s marking
and detection obligations for generative systems placed on the market or put into service before
2 August 2026, with conformity required by **2 December 2026**.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

The starting rule remains that all in-scope systems must comply with Article 50 on 2 August 2026,
regardless of when they were placed on the market or put into service.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), pp. 49–50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

For a system that is partly interactive and partly generative, the transition applies only to
Article 50(2); the Article 50(1) interaction disclosure still applies from 2 August 2026.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

## Pre-existing content

The system transition and the treatment of existing content are separate questions.
[Commission guidelines, paragraphs (153)–(154), pp. 49–50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

Outputs within Article 50(2) and deepfakes within Article 50(4) that were generated or
manipulated before 2 August 2026 do not require retroactive marking or labelling.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (154), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

The same non-retroactivity applies to public-interest text only when it was both generated or
manipulated and published before 2 August 2026.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (154), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

Text generated or manipulated before that date but published on or after 2 August 2026 must be
labelled.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (154), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

## Penalty exposure

Article 50 non-compliance falls under Article 99(4): administrative fines can reach **EUR 15
million** or, for an undertaking, **3% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding
financial year**, whichever is higher.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 99(4)(g), OJ pp. 115–116](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

For SMEs, including start-ups, the applicable cap is the percentage or fixed amount, whichever is
lower.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 99(6), OJ p. 116](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Union institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies can face administrative fines up to **EUR
750,000** for non-compliance with requirements other than the prohibited practices in Article 5.
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 100(3), OJ p. 117](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

## Source status

The Commission published the final guidelines on 20 July 2026 and adopted them to provide
practical guidance on Article 50.
[Commission guidelines landing page, “Publication 20 July 2026” and introductory text](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems)

The guidelines are non-binding; the Court of Justice of the European Union remains the body that
can ultimately give an authoritative interpretation of the AI Act.
[Commission guidelines, paragraph (5), p. 3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)

## Open questions for the operator

- **Headline terminology:** keep the precise “machine-readable marking and detectability duty,”
  or use the Commission quick-facts shorthand “marking obligation”? The guidelines support the
  longer form.
  [Commission guidelines, paragraph (153), p. 50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)
- **Existing-content detail:** keep the pre-existing-content section, or cut it to make the
  August-versus-December correction shorter? The distinction is supported, but it is separate
  from the system transition.
  [Commission guidelines, paragraphs (153)–(154), pp. 49–50](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)
- **Dropped prevalence claim:** this draft does not call the broad-deferral reading “widely
  repeated.” The three primary sources state the rule but do not measure the prevalence of that
  reading.
  [Commission guidelines](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215)
- **Closing line:** keep the published explainer vendor-neutral, or add a separately verified
  Caisson line after the publication surface is locked? This draft makes no Caisson capability
  claim.
