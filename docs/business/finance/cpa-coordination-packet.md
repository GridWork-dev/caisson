## 6. Pre-execution CPA modeling requested

For the structure counsel proposes, please provide a short written comparison covering at least the following.

### 6.1 Transfer, gift, valuation, and basis

- Counsel should identify the legal transferor, trustee, beneficiary, property transferred, delivery, retained powers, and effective date. The CPA should then determine whether the completed legal acts constitute a completed gift for federal tax purposes, identify the donor and reported donee, and state the required reporting.
- Determine fair market value using only assets and enforceable rights legally owned or held by Caisson on the valuation date. Do not include Liam's code, documentation, domains, or other IP as company property without chain-of-title evidence. Separately identify the effect of any IP assignment executed before, at, or after trust funding.
- Whether Form 709 is required or advisable and what attachments are needed for adequate disclosure.
- Donor basis, donee or trust basis, any dual-basis issue, gift-tax basis adjustment, and the records that must be preserved.
- Any generation-skipping transfer analysis that the proposed trust terms require.
- Model the gift, grantor-trust, estate-inclusion, basis, and valuation effects of Liam's vested interest being subject to divestment if he dies before the fixed handoff, with the interest then shifting to Lakshmi. Also model Lakshmi's simultaneous roles as settlor, trustee, and manager and Vik's successor roles.

### 6.2 Trust income-tax ownership

- Whether the proposed trust would be wholly or partly a grantor trust, a non-grantor trust, or treated as owned by another person under Internal Revenue Code section 678.
- Distinguish the Georgia-law member, the trustee holding legal title, the trust beneficiary, and the person treated as owner for federal income-tax purposes; do not infer tax ownership from any one label.
- Who would report Caisson's income, deductions, credits, and self-employment or other employment-tax items while the LLC remains a disregarded entity, if applicable.
- Whether Form 1041, grantor-trust reporting, Schedule C, estimated payments, or beneficiary reporting would apply.
- How distributions, retained income, expenses, and later transfer of the LLC interest should be tracked.
- Model the August 19, 2027 trust distribution of the LLC interest and Liam's member admission as separate legal steps that may have coordinated but distinct tax and reporting effects.
- Whether income allocated or distributed to Liam could be unearned income for Form 8615 purposes, including the effect of his age, student status, earned income, and support. Do not assume that all business income or trust income receives one character.

### 6.3 Payroll and Georgia consequences

- Identify the employer and payroll start date if Liam becomes an employee.
- Determine federal income-tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare, FUTA, and information-reporting treatment under the final ownership and tax structure.
- Determine Georgia withholding, unemployment-insurance, wage-reporting, and other employer registrations.
- Confirm a supportable wage method based on actual duties, time or task records, and comparable compensation.
- Keep wages separate from gifts, trust distributions, member distributions, and expense reimbursements.

A full-member trust may change the analysis of the parent-child employment-tax exception. The exception should not be assumed merely because Lakshmi is Liam's mother or because the LLC might otherwise be disregarded. The CPA should analyze the actual tax owner, trust terms, entity classification, and employer at the time wages begin.

### 6.4 Entity classification and S corporation caution

No corporate-classification or S corporation election was included in the reviewed records. The current instruction is that no S election is authorized pending written counsel and CPA advice. Please confirm Caisson's current federal and Georgia classification and do not recommend a later S election until counsel has resolved the ownership and trust structure and the CPA has confirmed:

- That the actual shareholder or deemed owner is eligible.
- That the trust qualifies under an available S corporation trust category, if applicable.
- That every required shareholder, beneficiary, trustee, or deemed-owner election and consent can be completed timely.
- That payroll, reasonable-compensation, distribution, basis, and Georgia consequences support the election.

The working position is an S election hold. Any later recommendation must be a separate written decision after trust eligibility, deemed ownership, payroll, reasonable compensation, basis, distributions, and Georgia consequences are resolved.
