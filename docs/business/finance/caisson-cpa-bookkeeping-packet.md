> This packet organizes source facts, open questions, and requested advisor work. It does not select a tax treatment, determine whether an expenditure is deductible, establish that a transfer was a gift or loan, or replace legal or tax advice.

## 1. Executive briefing

Caisson Software LLC is a newly formed, pre-sale Georgia software company. Its historical startup activity was paid from two mixed-use personal account environments: an American Express account under Liam Thadani's father that the father pays, and a Current debit account Liam describes as his own that was funded principally by his father. Both environments contain business-related and personal activity. Neither should be imported into the company books without a transaction-level reconstruction.

The immediate CPA objective is to create a traceable opening record without forcing uncertain family transfers into a predetermined tax label. The work should preserve the original account data, distinguish the person who paid from the person or entity that benefited, and keep three questions separate:

1. Was the purchase actually for Caisson?
2. What accounting treatment, if any, applies to the purchase?
3. How should the funding relationship among the father, mother, Liam, and LLC be characterized?

Those questions may have different answers. A business-purpose purchase does not establish that the payer made a loan, capital contribution, gift, or reimbursable advance. A family transfer does not establish compensation. A project-related cost is not automatically deductible.

### Requested outcome

The CPA or bookkeeper should deliver a supportable historical ledger, an exceptions queue, a documented opening-books method, recommendations for payroll and reimbursement controls, and a reserve model suitable for approval before the first sale. Counsel should resolve ownership, authority, minor-employment documentation, and the planned transfer of the LLC interest; the CPA should coordinate the tax and reporting consequences without treating the family's intended result as already implemented.

## 7. Reconstruction workflow

### Step 1 - Preserve and reconcile the sources

- Export all available transactions from January 1, 2025 onward.
- Record the data cutoff date and whether pagination, pending items, or connection limits create gaps.
- Reconcile transaction counts and statement totals where statements are available.
- Preserve source statements separately from the working workbook.
- Detect duplicate imports without deleting either source row.

### Step 2 - Link mechanical relationships

- Pair transfers only when amount, timing, direction, and account data support the match.
- Link refunds and reversals to the original charge.
- Distinguish a card payment from the underlying purchases.
- Group recurring subscriptions and installment-plan components.
- Flag, but do not automatically collapse, similar merchant/amount/date transactions.

### Step 3 - Classify four dimensions independently

1. **Business status:** business, personal, mixed, or uncertain.
2. **Accounting nature:** expense, startup or organizational candidate, asset, subscription, deposit, transfer, refund, or other.
3. **Funding relationship:** payer and possible reimbursement, loan, contribution, gift, or other pathway.
4. **Tax treatment:** reserved for the CPA and left unapproved until the facts and entity posture are confirmed.

### Step 4 - Review exceptions

Food, fuel, general shopping, cash withdrawals, family transfers, large deposits, and mixed-purpose purchases should default to personal or uncertain unless documentation supports a different result. Merchant name alone is not a business purpose.

### Step 5 - Approve and post

Liam may confirm factual purpose and identify receipts, but the CPA or authorized bookkeeper should approve posting treatment. Funding, gift, compensation, ownership, and tax conclusions require the appropriate adult client and advisor approval.

## 8. Classification framework for the CPA

The following are possible review labels, not conclusions and not a menu from which a desired tax result may be selected.

### 8.2 Accounting-nature candidates

- Organizational cost candidate.
- Pre-opening startup cost candidate.
- Current operating expense candidate.
- Capital asset or equipment candidate.
- Software, cloud, domain, or recurring subscription.
- Refund, statement credit, rebate, or reversal.
- Transfer or card payment that is not itself an expense.
- Personal or noncompany amount.
- Unknown or insufficiently documented.

The CPA should determine capitalization, amortization, depreciation, placed-in-service dates, elections, and deductibility. The January 2026 start of project spending, July 6 entity formation, and future first-sale or operational dates may be different legal and tax milestones.

### 8.3 Funding-path candidates

| Candidate | Facts needed before use |
|---|---|
| Father-to-LLC loan or advance | Intent to repay, borrower authority, terms, records, and counsel/CPA approval. |
| Amount reimbursable to father | Valid company expense, proof of payment, reimbursement policy or approved process, and no duplicate benefit. |
| Direct third-party payment on behalf of LLC | Evidence that Caisson incurred or accepted the obligation and the payment was for Caisson. Further balance-sheet treatment still required. |
| Gift to mother followed by member contribution | Evidence of two distinct intended events, donor and recipient intent, control of funds or property, and any reporting analysis. Do not collapse into one event without support. |
| Mother's member capital contribution | Evidence that the mother contributed cash or property in her capacity as current member. |
| Liam-paid reimbursable amount | Valid company expense, Liam as actual payer, documentation, and an approved reimbursement route. |
| Personal gift to Liam | Donative intent and personal transfer facts; does not make later spending a company expense or compensation. |
| Compensation to Liam | Actual services, employer authorization, payroll and labor-law compliance, and wage records. Informal allowance or gifts must not be relabeled as wages after the fact without advisor support. |
| Personal or family expenditure | No company posting except, if necessary, through a clearing record that is promptly resolved under CPA direction. |
| Unknown | Insufficient facts; hold outside final books or in a clearly identified suspense workflow approved by the CPA. |

### 8.4 Key principle for mixed accounts

Record the expense question and the funding question separately. For example, a documented Caisson software purchase on the father's card may be a credible business expenditure while the resulting amount payable, contribution, gift, or reimbursement remains unresolved. Conversely, a transfer described as a gift may be entirely personal even if another transaction later supported Caisson.

## 9. Minor services, gifts, payroll, and reimbursements

Liam is a minor, the reported principal code author, and the intended day-to-day operator. The family also reports regular support and gifts from his father. These facts create separate workstreams.

### CPA decisions requested

1. Determine who would be the employer if Liam becomes an employee and when employment should begin.
2. Confirm the LLC's tax classification and whether any federal family-employment rules apply to this exact mother-owned LLC structure. Do not assume an exception from the family relationship alone.
3. Identify federal and Georgia registrations, payroll filings, withholding, wage-payment, and recordkeeping requirements.
4. Coordinate with counsel on the current Georgia baseline for a 16-year-old, the parent-owned-business exception, hazardous/prohibited duties, any school or platform conditions, job description, authority, supervision, confidentiality, and invention assignment. Current Georgia guidance says work permits and work-hour limits do not apply after age 16, but the advisor should confirm the exact arrangement rather than carry forward rules for younger minors.
5. Establish a defensible compensation method based on actual services, contemporaneous time or task records, and approved payroll.
6. Keep wages, expense reimbursements, owner/member transactions, and personal gifts in separate ledgers and bank flows.
7. Determine whether an accountable reimbursement arrangement or another documented process should be adopted and from what prospective date.
8. Identify whether any historical amounts should be reimbursed and to whom; do not manufacture retroactive payroll or reimbursement records.

The 2026 federal annual gift-tax exclusion does not decide whether a payment was a gift, compensation, reimbursement, or business funding. Gift reporting and the planned LLC-interest transfer should be analyzed from the actual facts in coordination with counsel.

## 13. Tax-reserve model

No universal reserve percentage is proposed. The reserve workbook should separate cash planning from the tax return and use CPA-approved inputs.

### Model inputs

- Expected Paddle supplier earnings and payout timing.
- Refund, chargeback, and payout-adjustment exposure.
- Approved deductible operating expenses.
- CPA treatment of startup, organizational, asset, and subscription costs.
- Compensation, payroll tax, withholding, and benefit assumptions.
- Mother's applicable federal tax posture if the LLC is disregarded, or the entity's posture if another classification is selected.
- Self-employment tax or other employment-tax treatment.
- Georgia income and business-tax considerations.
- Prior-year safe-harbor data and payments already made.
- The tax effect, if any, of related-party funding and the planned LLC-interest transfer.

### Required scenario design

Create low, base, and high cases with visible, editable assumptions. Every rate must be marked **provisional - CPA approval required** until approved. If the CPA has not supplied a rate, leave the input blank or label it **CPA input required** rather than invent a precise percentage.

Keep three balances separate:

1. Estimated-tax reserve.
2. Refund and chargeback liquidity reserve.
3. General operating runway.

The model should show the calculation bridge from projected supplier proceeds to estimated taxable income and then to the cash-reserve target. It should also show what facts or elections could change the result.
